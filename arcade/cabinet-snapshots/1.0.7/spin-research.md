# Cornhole spin research — 2026-11-14

Flat spin means horizontal rotation that helps a bag fly level. An airmail goes directly into the hole without board contact; it can also use flat spin. A slider lands short and slides into the hole. A slide-in is sometimes loosely called a roll-in, but a true roll shot tips over a blocker.

Slick fabric slides farther and supports push shots. Sticky fabric stops sooner and supports blocks. Push-throughs transfer momentum into blockers, collecting bags into the hole or knocking them away. Real cuts/hooks depend on landing tilt, fabric, bag construction and uneven contact as well as rotation; spin direction alone does not guarantee a universal hook direction.

Arcade convention: A/back-left yields negative angular spin, clockwise from above, and rightward (+X) curl. C/back-right yields positive spin, counterclockwise, and leftward curl. This predictable convention is a gameplay approximation. Slick curl is stronger and decays more slowly than sticky curl; straight/no backswing has no curl. A six-inch-right landing needs C/left curl to reach the hole. Airmails require no surface curl. Existing blocker impulses remain active.
